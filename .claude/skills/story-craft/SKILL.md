# Story craft: tell it, don't report it

A Short lives or dies in its telling. A summary of a Hadith turned into scenes ("ek aadmi tha, usne yeh kiya,
phir yeh hua") is a report, not a story. Do this pass on every script (also the owner's own), then show the
owner the improved script + drift list before building. Reply in short Hinglish.

## 1. Read the whole source, not a summary
Find the full text of the Hadith (Bukhari/Muslim number given; if only a topic, find the reference and say which).
The best material hides in details that summaries (and GPT) drop. Answer these five every time:
1. **Who tells it, and to whom?** (A frame: "she told Aisha (RA) this story years later" is a ready-made structure.)
2. **Which sentence is repeated or quoted?** (A line someone kept saying = the hook AND the ending.)
3. **What happens after the "end"?** (Islam accepted, a habit for life, a dua answered later.)
4. **Who suffers most / who is weakest?** (A freed slave, a thirsty animal, a debtor: tell it from their side.)
5. **What is the strangest detail?** (A kite takes leather for meat, money in a log, a dog drinking from a shoe.)
Every answer that the script ignores is a drift to point out and usually the fix.

## 2. Hook: an open question in the first 3 seconds
The viewer must want an answer the video gives only near the end. Three kinds that work:
- **A strange sentence**: "Patti wala din mere Rab ka ajooba tha." (What day? What patti?)
- **An upside-down result**: "Lakdi mein hazaar dinar?" / "Ek billi… aur jahannam?"
- **A hidden thing**: a shadow passes, cut, the thing is gone.
Never open with the moral, the setting ("Bahut pehle ki baat hai…") or the reference.

## 3. Pick a structure (vary it from the last films)
- **Flash-forward**: show the most curious moment, then "Kahani shuru hoti hai…" (samundar-mein-1000-dinar).
- **Frame story**: someone tells it years later; we go back; we return to them at the end (laal patti).
- **Three tries**: three attempts/three people, each raising the stakes (gufa-ke-teen).
- **Mystery and replay**: the first shot is shown incomplete, at the reveal the same shot plays again in full
  (camera goes up: the kite). The replay is the strongest twist we have.
- **Countdown/clock**: a deadline the viewer can feel (the loan date, the thirst).
Write which one in the script header.

## 4. Narration that sounds told
- Short sentences, spoken words; one new surprising detail per scene; one colour or sound word
  (laal chamda, aari ki khar-khar) so the ear sees it.
- End each scene on an unfinished thought that the cut answers ("…aur patti gaayab thi.").
- Talk to the viewer at most once or twice ("Socho…", "Pata hai kya hua?"), never every scene.
- Banned: empty bridges ("Waqt guzarta gaya."), lecture lines in the middle, the moral before the end.
- Feelings through actions and faces, not adjectives ("uski aankhen bhar aayin" > "woh bahut dukhi thi").
- Keep every spoken sentence from the source as the source says it (dialogue only from the source; the
  narrator may quote a person who is not shown).

## 5. Ending: the source's own words
The strongest last line is usually inside the Hadith (a quoted sentence, the Prophet's ﷺ conclusion as narration,
the repeated line). Narrator says it, then the 3-6 word end line on screen (+ small reference), then a comment
question that the viewer can answer from their own life.

## 6. Score before building (0-2 each, total 10)
| | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | setting/moral first | interesting | open question in 3 s |
| Open loop | none | answered early | answered near the end |
| Heart | nobody to feel for | feelings told | we live it with one person |
| Twist / reveal | none | told | shown (replay, cut, close-up) |
| Ending | invented moral | source paraphrased | source's own words + end line |

## 7. Example: before / after (Sahih al-Bukhari 439)
- **Before (GPT):** "Ek qabeele ki ladki ki laal patti gum ho gayi. Logon ne ek aurat par ilzaam lagaya. Phir patti
  upar se giri. Woh beqasoor thi. Ilzaam se pehle saboot dekho." (Score 4: no frame, no heart, no ending.)
- **After:** Hook: a red patti on the sand, a shadow, cut, gone. Frame: in the Masjid of Madinah a woman keeps
  saying "Patti wala din mere Rab ka ajooba tha"; Aisha (RA) asks why (not shown). Heart: she was a freed
  slave who stayed with the tribe, so suspicion fell on her; a humiliating search (no details shown). Reveal: while
  she stands among them the kite drops the patti; replay of the first shot, camera rises to the kite. Her words:
  "Yahi hai jiska tumne mujh par ilzaam lagaya tha… main beqasoor thi." Ending: she came to Rasoolullah ﷺ and
  accepted Islam; her line "usi ne mujhe kufr ki basti se nijaat di"; end line "Ilzaam se pehle saboot dekho".
  (Score 10.)

## 8. Islamic limits stay first
Never show Allah, Prophets, angels or named Companions (not even voice). Angels in a story (e.g. the leper, bald
and blind man) are told by narration and the men's reactions only. Violence and private details are never shown.
Say clearly which parts are tasawwur.
